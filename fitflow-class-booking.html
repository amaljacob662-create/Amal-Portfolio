FitFlow — Class booking app

  • UX Design, User Flow, Wireframing
  • Independent project
  • 2026

Project info

FitFlow is a fictional, self-initiated 2026 concept exploring how a gym app could make booking classes faster by redesigning confusing user flows.

The focus was on simplifying navigation, improving structure, and creating a more intuitive experience for both members and guest visitors.

My role
UX/UI Designer (solo)
Context
Independent concept project
Year
2026
Tools
Figma, FigJam

01 — Overview

A concept gym app where members and guests can find and book a class quickly. Purpose: remove the friction that makes people give up before booking.

02 — The problem

  • Members had to log in before seeing the schedule
  • Guests could not find day-pass options
  • Six steps before reaching 'Book class'
  • Inconsistent buttons and unclear hierarchy

03 — Research and insights

These are concept assumptions, not findings from completed interviews or usability tests. The schedule-first approach still needs validation with real gym-goers.

  • Design hypothesis: show class times before asking users to log in
  • Design hypothesis: make day-pass options visible to guests
  • Design hypothesis: a login-first experience can feel like 'members only'

04 — Design process

  1. Open app→
  2. Log in→
  3. Main menu→
  4. Find schedule→
  5. Pick class→
  6. Confirm

Original flow (above) vs. reworked flow: Open app → Class schedule → Join now.

I iterated on the guest home screen three times, merging 'Gym vouchers', 'Memberships' and 'View classes' into one clear list.

05 — Final solution

A calm navy palette for focus, large touch targets for use mid-workout, and a persistent 'Join now' button so the main action is always one tap away.

06 — Reflection

The proposed booking path goes from six steps to three. This is a comparison of concept flows, not a measured usability result.

Next: test with real gym members to check whether they can book without help, and explore waitlist notifications.
